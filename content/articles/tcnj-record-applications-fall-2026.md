The College of New Jersey received a record 18,400 applications for its fall 2026 incoming class, a nearly 15 percent increase over last year that admissions officials say reflects the school's growing reputation as a high-value public alternative to private liberal arts colleges.

TCNJ has long marketed itself as a "public Ivy," emphasizing small class sizes and strong graduate outcomes at a fraction of private-school tuition. This year's application surge was concentrated in the college's nursing, computer science, and business programs, according to data released by the admissions office.

"Families are doing the math more carefully than they used to, and that math increasingly favors us," said TCNJ Vice President for Enrollment Management Lauren Vanderhoof. "We're seeing students who would have automatically applied to a private school five years ago now putting us at the top of their list."

The college says it will not significantly expand its incoming class size despite the application surge, a decision administrators frame as protecting the small-classroom experience central to the school's brand. That means this year's acceptance rate is expected to fall below 40 percent for the first time in TCNJ's history.

Officials also credited a statewide tuition freeze policy and expanded need-based aid for New Jersey residents as factors driving in-state interest, noting that roughly 80 percent of this year's applicant pool came from within New Jersey.

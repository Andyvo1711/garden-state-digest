Michael Grant, founder of development firm Shoreline Partners, broke ground this week on a mixed-use waterfront project aimed at revitalizing a long-underused stretch of the Jersey Shore that has sat mostly vacant since a previous development effort collapsed more than a decade ago.

The project will include ground-floor retail, a public boardwalk extension, and residential units above street level, replacing a series of abandoned lots that have drawn complaints from neighboring business owners for years. Grant says the project was structured in phases specifically to avoid the funding gaps that doomed the site's previous redevelopment attempt.

"The last group that tried to build here ran out of money halfway through and left the town with a half-finished eyesore for ten years," Grant said. "We phased this project so that each stage is financially self-sustaining before we break ground on the next one. It's slower, but it's the only way I'd stake my company's name on a site with this history."

Shoreline Partners secured financing through a combination of private equity investment and a state coastal redevelopment grant tied to public boardwalk access requirements. The first phase, expected to be complete within 18 months, will include the retail and boardwalk components, with residential units following in a second phase.

Local business owners have cautiously welcomed the project, though several told town officials they are waiting to see visible progress before fully believing this redevelopment attempt will succeed where the last one failed.

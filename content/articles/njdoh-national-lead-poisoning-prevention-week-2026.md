The New Jersey Department of Health marked National Lead Poisoning Prevention Week (October 25–31) with the theme "Keep Kids Safe from Lead."

The share of New Jersey children under six with elevated blood lead levels fell from 2.1% in state fiscal year 2020 to 1.4% in fiscal year 2024. The state awarded $15 million for fiscal 2027 for local screening, case management and home hazard investigations. New Jersey requires blood lead screening at 12 and 24 months, and insurers must cover screenings for children under 17.

Sources of exposure include lead paint in homes built before 1978, leaded pipes, contaminated soil, imported cosmetics, candies and spices, and metal cookware. "No amount of blood lead level is safe," Commissioner Raynard Washington said. The NJ Poison Center can be reached at 1-800-222-1222.

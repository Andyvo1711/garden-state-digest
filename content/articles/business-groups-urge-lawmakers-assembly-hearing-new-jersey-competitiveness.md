Leaders of the state's major business groups told the Assembly Commerce and Economic Development Committee on October 5 that New Jersey must act to stay competitive, the Jersey Vindicator reported.

NJ Chamber of Commerce president Tom Bracken cited the state's 50th-place business-friendliness ranking from CNBC and called for strategies that are "bold, aggressive, collaborative." Michele Siekerka of the New Jersey Business & Industry Association urged revisiting the independent-contractor rules and letting the corporate transit fee expire, saying business needs "predictability and certainty." The fee adds 2.5% for corporations with more than $10 million in taxable net income, for a combined 11.5% rate, and is scheduled to expire after 2028.

Other testimony included Debbie Hart of BioNJ on funding the Commission on Science, Innovation and Technology, Paul Rozenberg of the Shipping Association on a statewide supply chain council, and Daniel Klim of the restaurant association on average margins of about 2.8%. Committee chair William Spearman (D-Camden) said the panel would work with the Sherrill administration; Republican Brian Bergen called the hearing a "farce."

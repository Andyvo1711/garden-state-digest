Gov. Mikie Sherrill visited Cherry Hill High School East to hear from students and educators about New Jersey's school cellphone law, which requires every public school district to restrict student phone use throughout the school day beginning with the 2026–2027 school year.

Former Gov. Phil Murphy signed the law in January 2026. Districts decide how devices are stored, with options including lockers, locked pouches or classroom storage.

"We know that when kids put down the screens, their mental health and academic performance improve," Sherrill said. Education Commissioner Dr. Lily Laux said schools are seeing positive results, with classrooms reported as more focused. Superintendent Dr. Kwame Morton said staff and students report more face-to-face conversation, and junior Giovannina Viglietta said she is "much more productive" during the day.

The governor's office reported qualitative rather than statistical results, including fewer distractions and a livelier atmosphere in hallways and lunchrooms.

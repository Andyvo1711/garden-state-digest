Gov. Mikie Sherrill on July 30 signed legislation directing the state's education commissioner to recommend spring break dates for New Jersey's public school districts every year. The measure, S170, became P.L.2026, c.56.

Districts that schedule a spring break must consider the recommendation when building their academic calendars, though they are not required to adopt it, and the law does not require every district to have a spring break. The commissioner's recommended dates are due by June 30 before the school year they apply to, and the law takes effect with the first full school year after enactment.

The bill was sponsored by Sen. Patrick Diegnan (D-Middlesex) and Senate Majority Leader M. Teresa Ruiz (D-Essex/Hudson). Diegnan said mismatched breaks make it difficult to coordinate childcare or family trips, while Ruiz said the bill "paves the way for the increase in predictability" families need.

Supporters say the goal is to encourage neighboring districts to line up their calendars while leaving final decisions with local boards of education.

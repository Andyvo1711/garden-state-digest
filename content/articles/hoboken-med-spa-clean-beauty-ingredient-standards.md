A Hoboken med spa founder has become a vocal advocate for stricter clean beauty ingredient disclosure standards, building her business's entire brand identity around full transparency about product sourcing and ingredient origin at a time when "clean beauty" marketing claims have drawn increasing skepticism from consumers.

The spa publishes a full ingredient breakdown for every product used in its treatments, including sourcing information that goes beyond what regulations currently require. The founder says this approach was a deliberate response to what she describes as widespread greenwashing in the beauty industry, where products are marketed as "clean" or "natural" without any standardized definition behind those claims.

"The term 'clean beauty' means almost nothing right now because there's no legal definition attached to it, so any company can slap it on a label," the founder said. "We decided the only credible response was radical transparency — publish everything, let clients draw their own conclusions, and stop asking them to just trust a marketing label."

The spa's transparency-focused approach has drawn a loyal client base willing to pay premium prices for products with fully disclosed ingredient sourcing, though the founder acknowledges the model requires more supplier vetting than a typical med spa's purchasing process. She has also begun advocating publicly for state-level ingredient disclosure standards that would apply industry-wide rather than relying on individual businesses to self-regulate.

Industry observers say growing consumer skepticism toward vague "clean beauty" marketing claims is likely to push more spas toward similar transparency practices over time.

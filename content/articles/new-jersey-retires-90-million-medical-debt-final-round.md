New Jersey's Office of Healthcare Affordability and Transparency announced September 17 the final round of its medical debt relief program, retiring $90 million owed by nearly 20,000 residents, in partnership with the nonprofit Undue Medical Debt.

Since 2023, the state has retired more than $1.6 billion in medical debt for nearly 900,000 residents. Residents qualified if their income was at or below 400% of the federal poverty line or their medical debt equaled 5% or more of annual income. No action is needed: residents cannot apply, and Undue-branded letters are being mailed.

The release notes that nearly 1 in 7 New Jersey residents lived in families whose out-of-pocket health spending exceeded 10% of income in 2024. "Medical debt is a symptom of the problem in our healthcare system," Health Commissioner Raynard Washington said.

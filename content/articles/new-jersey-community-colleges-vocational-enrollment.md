Enrollment in vocational and trade programs across New Jersey's community colleges has climbed sharply over the past two admissions cycles, according to data compiled by the New Jersey Council of County Colleges, as more students weigh skilled trade careers against the rising cost of four-year degrees.

Electrical, HVAC, welding, and advanced manufacturing programs have seen the steepest growth, with several colleges reporting waitlists for the first time in years. Administrators say the shift is being driven both by cost concerns and by a tightening labor market for skilled trades, where retiring workers are leaving openings that employers are struggling to fill.

"Parents who spent the last twenty years pushing every kid toward a four-year degree are starting to ask harder questions about return on investment," said Council of County Colleges spokesperson Denise Marlowe. "A two-year electrical program that ends in a union apprenticeship and a starting wage above $28 an hour is a very compelling alternative when a four-year degree can leave a graduate with six figures of debt and no guaranteed job."

Several colleges have responded by expanding lab capacity and adding evening cohorts to accommodate working adults switching careers. Community college officials say roughly a third of new vocational program enrollees already hold some college credit or a prior bachelor's degree in an unrelated field.

Statewide workforce projections cited by the council suggest demand for licensed electricians and HVAC technicians in New Jersey will continue to outpace the supply of trained workers through at least the next decade.

Several major New Jersey hospital systems announced expanded preventive care outreach and vaccination clinics this month ahead of the upcoming flu season, targeting communities that have historically reported lower vaccination rates and higher rates of flu-related hospitalization.

The coordinated push includes pop-up vaccination clinics at community centers, libraries, and senior housing complexes, along with expanded weekend hours at primary care practices affiliated with the participating hospital systems. Public health officials involved in the effort say the outreach specifically targets neighborhoods identified through prior-year hospitalization data as having lower vaccination uptake.

"Flu hospitalizations aren't distributed evenly across the state — they cluster in specific communities, often where access to a regular primary care doctor is more limited," said a New Jersey Department of Health official involved in coordinating the effort. "Meeting people where they already are, rather than expecting them to seek out a clinic, has consistently proven to be the more effective strategy."

Hospital systems participating in the initiative say they are also emphasizing vaccination for high-risk groups, including patients over 65 and those with chronic respiratory conditions, who face significantly elevated risk of severe complications from seasonal flu.

Officials say final vaccination rate data from the initiative won't be available until after flu season concludes, but early clinic attendance figures suggest turnout is running ahead of the same period last year.

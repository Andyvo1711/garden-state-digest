Small business lending across New Jersey is rebounding this year as regional banks loosen credit standards that had tightened considerably in recent years, offering relief to business owners who had struggled to access capital for expansion or equipment purchases.

Banking industry data shows small business loan originations climbing across several New Jersey regional banks, a shift lenders attribute to improved economic conditions and reduced concern about loan default rates compared with prior years. Business owners who previously reported being turned down or offered unfavorable terms say they are now finding more competitive offers.

"Two years ago I couldn't get a straight answer from any lender about expanding my restaurant into a second location," said a Union County small business owner who recently secured financing for a second storefront. "This year I actually had two banks competing for my business, which felt like a different universe compared to where things stood before."

Regional bank executives say the lending rebound reflects both stronger small business balance sheets and increased competitive pressure among banks eager to grow their commercial loan portfolios after a period of caution. Community development officials note that lending growth has been uneven, with some underserved communities still reporting difficulty accessing affordable credit compared to wealthier areas.

Economists tracking the trend say continued small business lending growth will likely depend on broader interest rate conditions remaining stable over the coming year.

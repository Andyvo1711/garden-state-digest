Stevens Institute of Technology moved into the national top 50 for undergraduate engineering programs this year, according to newly released rankings, a jump the Hoboken school attributes to a multi-year push in faculty hiring and sponsored research funding.

University leadership pointed to a 35 percent increase in externally funded research over the past four years, much of it tied to systems engineering and maritime robotics work conducted along the Hudson waterfront campus. Stevens has also added 22 tenure-track engineering faculty positions since 2023, part of a strategic plan aimed at reducing class sizes in upper-level courses.

"Rankings are a lagging indicator of decisions we made years ago," said Stevens Institute President Nariman Farvardin in a statement. "What matters more is that our students are now working alongside faculty on funded research starting in their sophomore year instead of their senior year."

The school's location on the Hudson River continues to shape its research identity, with several labs focused on coastal infrastructure resilience — a subject with obvious relevance following repeated flooding events along the New Jersey waterfront in recent years. Stevens says enrollment in its civil and environmental engineering tracks has grown nearly 20 percent as a result.

Administrators say the ranking improvement is likely to boost applications for the fall 2027 cycle, though they caution that maintaining the gain will require continued investment in lab space, some of which remains constrained by the campus's compact Hoboken footprint.

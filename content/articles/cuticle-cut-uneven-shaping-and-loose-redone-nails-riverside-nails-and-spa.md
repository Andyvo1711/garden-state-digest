A customer visited Riverside Nails & Spa for a fill and began their review with a genuine compliment: the staff was kind. That warmth is a real strength and a solid foundation for any salon. The customer's account, however, describes a service that did not meet their expectations, with a cut cuticle, an unexpected nail shape, and redone nails that felt unstable.

## A Cuticle Cut During the Service

According to the customer, their cuticle was cut during the fill, and the team continued working on it while it was bleeding. Cuticle work calls for a light touch, and any nick that draws blood is a clear signal to pause. The recommended steps are to stop the service, clean the area, apply an antiseptic and a bandage if needed, let the customer know what happened, and ask how they would like to continue.

Customers often feel more at ease when the team is open about what has happened and shows care for the injury before returning to the service. A technician can also trim conservatively around the cuticle line, leaving a little more skin rather than risking a cut, and can switch to fresh, clean tools after any incident.

## Shape and Finish

The customer also felt the nail shape was unattractive, and compared the curved result to a bird's claw. Shape is one of the most visible parts of a fill, so it is worth confirming before filing begins. Technicians can ask what shape and length the customer prefers, and show a reference photo if one is available. Checking each nail from above and from the side, with all fingers side by side, helps catch uneven curves before the polish goes on.

## Redone Nails That Did Not Feel Secure

Two nails were redone, and the customer said they felt as if they might come off with any pressure. When a nail is redone, extra care is needed: the surface must be properly prepared, the product applied evenly, and the new layer cured fully. A quick test of firmness before the customer leaves, such as a gentle press at the edge of each redone nail, can confirm that it feels secure.

Offering a short follow-up window, such as inviting the customer to come back within a few days if a nail lifts, shows that the salon stands behind its work.

## Editorial Perspective

This case offers several lessons. First, pause the service immediately when a cuticle is cut, treat the area, and explain clearly what has happened before deciding how to continue. Second, confirm nail shape and length before filing begins, and check all nails together before moving to the final step. Third, test every redone nail for firmness and offer a follow-up window so that any concerns can be resolved quickly. A kind team is a real asset, and pairing it with careful technique and open communication gives customers every reason to return.

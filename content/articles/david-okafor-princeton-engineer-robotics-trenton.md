David Okafor, a Princeton-trained mechanical engineer, opened a new robotics manufacturing facility in Trenton this month for his company Ironrail Automation, which builds warehouse automation robots used for pallet handling and inventory movement.

Okafor spent nearly a decade working at established robotics firms before founding Ironrail four years ago, betting that a wave of manufacturers looking to reduce dependence on overseas supply chains would create sustained demand for domestically built automation hardware. The Trenton facility, a renovated former industrial building, will employ 80 people at full capacity, combining precision manufacturing roles with software engineering positions.

"Every warehouse operator I talk to has the same anxiety about supply chain disruption, and automation is one of the few tools that actually reduces their dependence on any single overseas supplier," Okafor said. "Building the robots themselves domestically was a logical next step, not just a patriotic talking point."

Ironrail chose Trenton in part due to available industrial real estate at a fraction of the cost of comparable space in North Jersey, along with state manufacturing tax incentives Okafor says made the location financially competitive despite being farther from major ports. The company has raised $22 million in venture funding to date.

Okafor says Ironrail's current order backlog extends roughly eight months, driven largely by mid-sized logistics companies seeking to automate operations without the multi-year capital commitments required by larger automation vendors.

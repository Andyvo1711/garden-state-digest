A Hoboken wellness studio has built a loyal following over the past year around contrast therapy sessions that pair infrared sauna heat with cold plunge tubs, drawing PATH train commuters who stop in before or after the workday for 45-minute sessions.

The studio offers timed sessions alternating between infrared sauna rooms heated well above traditional saunas and cold plunge tubs kept just above freezing, a combination proponents say improves circulation and reduces muscle soreness. Owners say the format has resonated particularly with Hoboken's dense population of young professionals looking for a wellness routine that fits into a tight commuting schedule.

"Our members aren't people with two free hours for a spa day, they're people squeezing in 45 minutes between the PATH train and their apartment," said the studio's founder. "The whole model is built around efficiency without giving up the actual physiological benefit people are looking for."

The studio has expanded its class schedule to include early morning and late evening slots to accommodate commuter demand, and membership has grown steadily through word of mouth rather than paid advertising. Local competitors have taken notice, with at least two similar contrast therapy studios opening in Hoboken and Jersey City over the past year.

Wellness industry observers say the format's popularity reflects a broader shift toward shorter, higher-intensity wellness routines that fit into commuter lifestyles rather than traditional half-day spa visits.

The New Jersey Department of Health on September 23 marked National Newborn Screening Awareness Month and Public Health Laboratory Appreciation Month, noting more than 60 years of universal newborn screening in the state.

Newborns are screened within 24 to 48 hours of birth for 61 disorders, up from 52 in 2012, through a hearing test, a pulse oximetry test and a heel-stick bloodspot test. New Jersey was the first state to require pulse oximetry screening for critical congenital heart defects. About 98,000 babies are born in the state each year, and about 1 in 350 is diagnosed with a condition on the panel.

The follow-up team reaches providers within three hours about critically abnormal results, covering more than 1,100 babies a year. "Newborn screening is a powerful tool that helps protect thousands of infants born in New Jersey each year," said Commissioner Raynard Washington.

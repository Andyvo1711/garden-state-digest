Princeton University celebrated the opening of its new Environmental Engineering Quad this week, a $220 million complex that consolidates climate-focused research programs that had previously been scattered across five separate buildings on campus.

The four-building complex includes dedicated wet labs for carbon capture materials research, a battery storage testing facility, and a data center built to model regional climate scenarios for the mid-Atlantic. University officials say the quad was designed specifically to pair engineering faculty with environmental science researchers on shared projects rather than siloed departmental work.

"We wanted a building that forces collaboration instead of just permitting it," said Dean of Engineering Priyanka Desai at Tuesday's ribbon-cutting. "The last decade of climate research showed us that the best breakthroughs happen at the seams between disciplines, and this building is built around those seams."

The project was funded through a mix of university endowment spending, a $60 million gift from an anonymous alumni family, and a $35 million state grant tied to New Jersey's clean energy workforce goals. State officials attended the opening and pointed to the facility as part of a broader push to keep climate-tech graduates working in New Jersey rather than relocating after graduation.

Roughly 40 faculty researchers and 300 graduate and undergraduate students will use the space starting this spring semester. The university says three new undergraduate courses tied to the facility's carbon capture labs will open for enrollment in the fall.

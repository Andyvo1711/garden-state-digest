Kroll Bond Rating Agency (KBRA) raised New Jersey's general obligation credit rating to AA- on September 3, the highest the agency has assigned the state since it began rating New Jersey in 2015, according to the governor's office.

Gov. Mikie Sherrill's office called it the first credit rating increase a New Jersey governor has received in their first year in office, and cited a budget that cut the structural deficit by more than half, a full pension payment, record property tax relief and school funding, and a $6 billion surplus. "In just 7 months, we've taken on the tough challenges and the status quo," Sherrill said.

ROI-NJ and Bond Buyer reported the AA- rating sits one level above the state's A+ ratings from S&P and Fitch and in line with Moody's Aa3, and that the administration counted it as New Jersey's 10th consecutive upgrade from the major rating agencies since the pandemic-era downgrades. Treasurer Aaron Binder credited the decision to reducing the structural deficit while keeping the full pension contribution.

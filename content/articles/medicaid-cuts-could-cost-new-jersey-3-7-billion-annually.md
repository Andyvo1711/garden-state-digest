State officials estimate that federal changes to Medicaid could cost New Jersey as much as $3.7 billion a year, according to New Jersey Monitor reporting on October 22.

Human Services Commissioner Stephen Cha said up to 330,000 people could be forced off their existing health plans, which would reduce Medicaid payments to doctors and hospitals by $2 billion to $3 billion annually.

Some changes have already taken effect. Starting October 1, refugees, humanitarian parolees and victims of trafficking and domestic violence, among other groups, no longer qualify for Medicaid, though children, pregnant people and certain other entrants still can. Cuts to hospital funding will be phased in over the coming years.

The changes come as hospitals and clinics brace for more uninsured patients.
